1. The embedding model only changed one part of the error, and it was the biggest part. All three systems missed almost exactly the same speech (about 11%) and added the same false speech (about 6%), because they share the segmentation model. Every difference between them sits in speaker confusion: 14.7% for TitaNet, 23.0% for ERes2Net, 41.2% for WeSpeaker. A single DER number would not tell a builder this; the breakdown says "your speech detection is fine, fix the voice model". This is why the leaderboard puts the error mix next to every score.
  2. One configuration knob mattered more than the choice of model. On the dev split, the sherpa-onnx default threshold (0.5) gave TitaNet 30.9% DER and ERes2Net 37.5%; the dev-picked 0.9 gave 21.7% and 22.9%. That 9–15 point swing from one setting is bigger than the 8.4-point test gap between the two best systems. If a leaderboard runs one system with a tuned config and another with defaults, it is ranking configurations, not models. Hence the rule: defaults plus one declared config, both published, tuned only on public dev data.
  3. The ranking was clear here, but single files disagreed with it. All three pairwise gaps are significant under the paired bootstrap, so the three systems would get three separate bands. Yet per file, TitaNet ranged from 18% to 50% DER, and on the 11-speaker VoxConverse clip ERes2Net beat TitaNet by 10 points. A leaderboard built from a handful of files, or one that lets a buyer sort by a single file type, can show a "winner" that loses overall. Recording-level resampling and rank bands are the guard.
  4. Legacy scoring hides a fifth of the error. The same TitaNet output scores {b_der}% under the proposed setting, {b_c25}% with a 0.25 s collar, {b_skip}% with overlap skipped, and {b_legacy}% with both (the setting many older papers quote). The collar alone removed more error than skipping overlap did, because these meetings have many short turns: TitaNet assigned only 31% of sub-second turns to the right speaker.
  5. Far-field audio hurt far more than the simulated phone line. On the same two AMI meetings, TitaNet went from {b_close_ami}% (headset mix) to {b_far_abs}% on the single far-field mic. The phone simulation mostly raised missed speech (TS3003a: 41 s missed on the headset mix, 101 s on the phone line), because quiet speech falls under the speech detector once the band is cut, while confusion actually dropped on both meetings. Two lessons: the phone track must be real phone audio, since my simulation is too gentle; and the condition breakdown should show error parts, not just DER.
  6. Speaker counting is the visible failure. Even the best system found the exact number of speakers in only 2 of 8 files (mean error {tit_count} speakers); WeSpeaker produced 21 "speakers" for a 4-person meeting. Giving systems the true count cut TitaNet's DER from {b_der}% to {orc_titanet}% and WeSpeaker's from {der_wespeaker}% to {orc_wespeaker}%, almost entirely through lower confusion. That gain is real for fixed-count products (two-party calls) and unavailable to meeting products, which is why count-hinted results live in a separate column and never in the rank.
  7. JER told a different story than DER. TitaNet beat ERes2Net by 8.4 DER points, but their JER was nearly the same ({jer_titanet} vs {jer_eres2net}): once every speaker counts equally, regardless of how long they talk, the gap almost closes. For a clinical buyer who cares about the quiet patient, that is a different ranking signal, which is why JER sits next to DER and is sortable.
  8. Two scorers agreed. pyannote.metrics and dscore matched to within {xcheck_max} DER points on every file, so the numbers above are not an artefact of one tool.

Caveats. This is a pilot, not a product ranking. The WeSpeaker result is about this particular ONNX export combined with sherpa-onnx's clustering; its dev curve was noisy across thresholds, which points to a calibration mismatch rather than a weak voice model, and it says nothing about pyannote's own 3.1 pipeline, which uses different clustering. Eight files from two corpora, with block-level rather than recording-level resampling, give intervals that are too narrow. And all three systems share one segmentation model, so this tests embeddings, not full pipelines.
